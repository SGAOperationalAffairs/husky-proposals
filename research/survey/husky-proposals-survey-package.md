# Husky Proposals: survey package

A student survey and an SGA leader survey to test the idea before building more of it. Draft for your review.

## What is in this package

- **Two surveys.** One for students and one for SGA leaders. Each has a core set of questions (about 7 minutes) and optional extras (about 10 minutes in total).
- **A script** that builds both as Google Forms in one run, so you do not have to type about 60 questions by hand.
- **Five screenshots** from the prototype to put in the forms.
- **A plan** for what each question decides, how to send the surveys, how to protect people's privacy, and how to read the results without fooling yourself.

## First: getting data, not just agreement

You said you need validation. The risk with a survey is that it gives you agreement, because the people who answer are the people who like the idea. Four things in this package guard against that:

1. **Questions where "no" is a real answer.** Every important question has an option for "I would not use this", "I do not want this", or "worse than today".
2. **Questions before the pitch.** Students answer how they share ideas today, and what gets in the way, before they see the idea. That tells you about the problem without priming them.
3. **Decision rules written down first.** The table below says what result would make you change course. Agree on it before sending, then do not move the line after you see the data.
4. **Choices, not just reactions.** Many questions ask people to choose between options (unlimited or limited votes, how a board should pick) instead of rating "do you like this?".

One caution. People say yes to "would you use this?" more often than they actually use things. Treat the likelihood question as an upper limit, not a forecast.

## What the survey should help you decide

The thresholds are starting points. Change them to what you actually believe before you send anything.

| Decision | Questions | What would change the plan |
|---|---|---|
| Do students want this? | S10, S11, S13, S14 | Under 3 in 10 rate 4 or 5 on "likely to use": rethink the concept. 3 to 5 in 10: read the open answers and fix the biggest worry before building. Over 5 in 10: continue. |
| Is "no limit on support" acceptable? | S15, S27, L25 | If most students prefer limited votes, or many worry a few people could inflate a count: add a vote cap or a "top pick." |
| How should boards choose from the list? | S17 and L17, S18 and L19 | If students and leaders rank the criteria very differently: publish the criteria and pick a rule both groups can defend. |
| How long will students wait? | S19, L10 | If most students will not wait over a month but initiatives take a semester: boards must post progress early, and the site must set expectations. |
| Will volunteers come, and can we handle them? | S11, S22, L12, L14 | If under 1 in 10 students would tap "I'll participate": the recruiting benefit is small. If leaders cannot reply within a week: lengthen the contact window or limit offers. |
| What privacy do students expect? | S25, S26, S27 | If most want counts only: show no names. If many would not post under their name: allow anonymous posts. |
| Do students trust SGA to act? | S6, S7 | If few believe SGA can get action: early visible wins matter more than features. Start with ideas that can be finished. |
| What evidence moves administration? | L22, L23, L24 | Use leaders' answers to decide what to show administration. If possible, check with an administrator you trust. |
| What support level makes an idea "ready for review"? | L18 | Use the middle answer for each size (small, medium, large) as the first threshold. Revisit after real data. |
| Is the leader design right? | L15, L16, L25, L26 | If leaders prefer a dashboard to notification badges, or rarely say they would use it weekly: simplify before building. |

## Sending it

### Who and how many

- **Students.** Aim for 300 or more responses. With that many you can compare a few groups. If the sample were random, 300 would give roughly a 6 point margin of error, but it will not be random, so treat percentages as rough. Fewer than 300 is still useful.
- **Leaders.** Try to hear from most of them. With a small group, report counts (for example "11 of 18") and never percentages of tiny groups.
- **Reach beyond the usual crowd.** The people most likely to answer are the people already engaged with SGA. To hear from everyone else, send it through channels SGA does not usually use: cultural and spiritual life centers, residence hall councils, club presidents, class group chats, the global campus network (through your Global Experience Committee), and co-op students by email.
- **Check who answered.** Compare your respondents by year and college against the real undergraduate population. If a group is missing, push specifically to reach it before closing.

### Timing

1. **Pilot first (a few days).** Have 5 students and 3 leaders take it while you watch, without helping. Time it. Note every place they hesitate or ask what a question means. Fix those.
2. **Open for about 2 weeks,** with one reminder in the middle. Avoid exam weeks.
3. **Analyze in the week after it closes.** Share results back within a few weeks. Telling students what you learned is the best way to earn answers next time.

### Invitation messages

**Students (Slack, email, Instagram story, group chats)**

> SGA is exploring a new way for students to post ideas for campus and see what happens to them. Before we build anything, we want to know what you think, including if you think it's a bad idea. 7 minutes, anonymous. [link]

**Leaders (Slack, Senate, board meetings)**

> Campus Affairs is designing a tool for posting student ideas, tracking support, and reaching volunteers. We need your honest input before we build more, especially if you think it won't work for your board. 7 minutes, anonymous. [link]

### In person

At tabling or events, hand students a tablet or a QR code and let them fill it out on the spot. This reaches people who would never open an email from SGA. The flyer in the prototype can be adapted for this.

## Privacy and data handling

- **Do not collect emails in the survey.** The form is built without them. If you turn on "limit to one response" in Google Forms, it forces a sign-in and records emails, so you lose anonymity. Accept a small risk of duplicates instead.
- **Use a separate form for contact details.** Put a link on the last page: "Want to hear what we learn, or join a short follow-up conversation? Leave your email here." Because it is a different form, nobody can connect an email to survey answers.
- **Report groups, not individuals.** Only report a breakdown (for example by year or college) when the group has 10 or more responses for students, or 5 for leaders.
- **Clean open-ended answers before sharing.** Remove names, specific people, and anything that could identify someone, as you would with the FIT survey data.
- **Store raw data in one place** with Campus Affairs only. Share summaries, not exports.
- **Check the rules first.** Ask your advisor whether anything is required to survey students, and what the rules are for prizes or incentives before you offer one.

## Reading the results

1. **Export to CSV** and keep an untouched copy.
2. **Start with the decision table above.** For each row, write down the result and the decision it triggers before you read anything else.
3. **Compare groups.** The most useful cuts are year, college, where students live, and where they are this semester (co-op or on campus). Also compare students with leaders on the matched questions: S17 and L17 (how to choose), S18 and L19 (small-group ideas).
4. **Read the open answers.** Group them into themes. Have a second person group them separately, then compare. The surprises are usually here.
5. **Report what surprised you,** including anything that goes against what you expected. If the survey only ever agrees with you, something is wrong with how it was run.
6. **Write down what you will change** because of the results, and what you will not, with a reason.

## How to build the forms

1. Go to script.google.com and create a new project.
2. Paste in the contents of `create-surveys.gs` and save.
3. Optional: set `CONFIG.coreOnly` to `false` for the full version (the default is the shorter core version), and paste the image IDs.
4. Choose `createSurveys` in the function menu and press Run. Approve the permissions.
5. Open View, then Execution log, for the links. Both forms also appear in your Drive.
6. Open each form once and check it, then send it to your pilot group.

I could not run the script against Google from here, so it has not been tested there. I did check that the code is valid and that it builds the right number of questions. If a line errors, send me the message and I will fix it. The only thing I expect might need a manual touch is the "use each rank once" limit on the ranking questions. If that does not apply, set it in the form: open the question, choose Response validation, and select "Limit to one response per column."

## Things to check before you send

- **College names and the "where are you this semester" options** match how students describe themselves.
- **The decision thresholds** in the table are numbers you believe.
- **Who owns the data** and where it is stored.
- **Your pilot group** has not already seen the prototype, so that their reactions are fresh.
- **Whether you will share results.** The confirmation message promises it. Only keep that line if you will.
