BYLAWS

ARTICLE I: MEMBERSHIP
Anyone shall be a member of the Association if they are an undergraduate student of Northeastern University and a member of:
a Committee;
a Board;
an Advisory Board;
a Working Group;
the Senate;
Leadership; or
the Executive Board.
A member of a Committee shall be defined as one that has attended a minimum of 25% of transpired Committee meetings for any given Committee in a given academic semester.
A Delegate shall be any member of the Association that is not a Senator or on the Executive Board.
An Active Member shall be anyone that has been a Member of the Association in the most recent semester, not counting intersessions.
All Executive Board members and Leadership Positions shall serve a term beginning on July 1st and extending to June 30th of the following year. If the date of appointment, confirmation, or election is after July 1st, the term begins on that date.
In order to be eligible for Association membership, a student must be enrolled as a Northeastern  student and be in good academic standing with the University.

ARTICLE II: OFFICE OF THE PRESIDENT
DIVISION RESPONSIBILITY
The Office of the President is in charge of leading the Association, interfacing with important administrators and governmental officials, serving as the judicial and electoral arms of the Association via the Appeals Board and Elections Board, and carrying out any Office of the President goals for the Student Body.
The Division will be overseen by the Student Body President and the Executive Vice President.

APPEALS BOARD
The Appeals Board will be chaired by the Parliamentarian.
The Board has the power and responsibility to:
Hear appeals of decisions of the Association’s Boards and Committees and alleged violations of the Association’s governing documents;
Maintain the right of every student to file an appeal;
Review all appeals without bias; and
Understand and enact the policies of the Appeals Manual.
The Board should consist, at minimum, of four justices and, at maximum, of six justices.
No justice may hold a position in any other branch of the Association while serving on the Board.
In the case that an inherent conflict exists, a justice must abstain from voting. Justices must also recuse themselves from an appeal if a conflict of interest exists and immediately inform the Parliamentarian no later than 24 hours before the appeal meeting is scheduled to take place. 
Each justice is expected to maintain personal confidentiality and be committed to ensuring appeals remain confidential.

ELECTIONS BOARD
The Elections Board will be chaired by the Director of Elections.
The Board has the power and responsibility to:
Ensure that all students have the opportunity to vote in student body elections;
Follow all aspects of the Presidential Elections Manual as closely and effectively as possible, to ensure a fair election process;
Communicate with the Student Body when the elections are happening, who the candidates in the election are, and how to vote in the election, through coordination with External Affairs;
Follow the Presidential Elections Manual procedures in considering whether referenda submissions are complete and ready for vetting;
Follow all aspects of the Presidential Elections Manual as closely and effectively as possible to ensure a fair and unbiased referenda vetting process, offering resources upon request to assist with referenda submissions;
Communicate with the Student Body through effective communication channels when the referenda submissions are available, how to submit a referendum petition, and what the process is to have a referendum submission be a part of that year’s Student Body Election, through coordination with External Affairs;
Ensure that all students have the opportunity to vote in Senate Elections;
Follow all aspects of the Senate Elections Manual as closely and effectively as possible to ensure fair election processes;
Communicate with the Student Body when the Senate Elections are happening, who the candidates in the Election are, and how to vote in the Election, through coordination with External Affairs;
Work with the Speaker of the Senate to run all internal elections;
Maintain the Direct Elections Manual and Senate Elections Manual, which will contain all the rules and procedures pertaining to the election of Student Body President, Executive Vice President, and Senators, subject to Senate approval;
Oversee the process by which students serve the Association through direct election, representative election, and petition; and
Inform the student body, through reports submitted at the end of each month of the semester on initiatives, projects, and relevant information for the student body.
The Board should consist, at maximum, of twelve members.
Membership will exclude the Student Body President, any member of the Appeals Board, any nominated candidate for Student Body President or Executive Vice President, and any candidate for Senatorship.
Members of the Board must sign an agreement to not run to be Student Body President, Executive Vice President, or a Senator during that academic year.

ARTICLE III: ACADEMIC AFFAIRS
DIVISION RESPONSIBILITY
The Division of Academic Affairs serves to address student concerns in the areas of college relations and curriculum, the Provost’s office, experiential education, libraries, classroom facilities, career services, honors programs, the University calendar, and undergraduate research.
The Division will be overseen by the Vice President of Academic Affairs.

ACADEMIC SERVICES COMMITTEE
The Academic Services Committee will be chaired by the Director of Academic Services.
The Committee has the power and responsibility to:
Work with university administrators to address student academic concerns;
Develop initiatives to enhance curriculum and experiential learning; and
Provide recommendations on policies affecting academic resources and student success.

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE ADVISORY BOARD
The Artificial Intelligence Advisory Board has the power and responsibility to:
Serve as the official liaison between the Northeastern University Student Body and all Northeastern administration in regards to Artificial Intelligence, including, but not limited to, the Center for Advancing Teaching and Learning Through Research (CATLR) and the Chief of Artificial Intelligence.
The Advisory Board will consist of eight at-large members and representatives from at least the following organizations:
Graduate Student Government; and
Artificial Intelligence Club of Northeastern University.

CO-OP WORKING GROUP
The Director of Co-op will serve as the Student Chair.
The Senior Director of Cooperative Education will serve as the Administrative Chair.
The Co-op Working Group has the power and responsibility to:
Meet with university administrators to discuss and provide valuable feedback on aspects of the co-op program; and
Serve as the connection between the student body, Employer Engagement and Career Design, and the Office of the Chancellor by distributing communications, surveys, and other information flows between the two.
The Working Group will consist of representatives from each undergraduate college.

SPECIALIZED LEADERSHIP POSITIONS
The College Liaisons, which shall consist of selected representatives from each of Northeastern’s undergraduate colleges, have the power and responsibility to:
Facilitate communication between students and college administration as well as ensure representation of student concerns in college decision-making processes;
Advocate for academic policies that enhance the student experience; and
Identify and address inconsistent practices among the colleges and advocate for college-specific issues.
The Faculty Senate Representative has the power and responsibility to:
Attend Faculty Senate meetings as a non-voting member and provide student perspectives on academic matters;
Serve as a liaison between the student body and faculty governance structures;
Advocate for student interests in faculty-led policy decisions and administrative changes; and
Collaborate with other Association Divisions on relevant projects initiated/need approved in the Faculty Senate.
The University Undergraduate Curriculum Committee Representative has the power and responsibility to:
Exercise voting rights on proposed curriculum changes and academic policies;
Advocate for student perspectives in curriculum development and university-wide academic initiatives; and
Collaborate with faculty and administration to improve course offerings and academic programming.

The Division will be overseen by the Vice President of Campus Affairs.

CAMPUS SERVICES COMMITTEE
The Campus Services Committee will be chaired by the Director of Campus Services.
The Committee has the power and responsibility to:
Advocate for improvements to any and all campus services, including campus recreation, transportation, public safety, dining, and housing.

SUSTAINABILITY COMMITTEE
The Sustainability Committee will be chaired by the Director of Sustainability.
The Committee has the power and responsibility to:
Advocate for sustainability initiatives and work with the Climate Justice and Sustainability Hub, among other administrative offices, to address student sustainability concerns on the Boston campus.

GREEN INITIATIVES BOARD
The Green Initiatives Board will be chaired by the Director of Green Initiatives.
The Board has the power and responsibility to:
Distribute available funds toward sustainability projects initiated by undergraduate students and organizations;
Pursue and secure external and internal funding sources to expand the Green Initiatives Fund, ensuring the long-term growth and financial viability of student-led sustainability efforts;
Initiate and maintain communication with university offices and administration, facilitating collaboration on university-wide initiatives related to sustainability and environmentalism; and
Understand and enact the policies of the Green Initiatives Manual.
The Board should consist, at maximum, of twelve members.

CAMPUS PLANNING ADVISORY BOARD
The Campus Planning Advisory Board has the power and responsibility to:
Serve as the official liaison between the Northeastern University student body and Campus Planning Team within Planning, Real Estate, and Facilities (PREF), such as the Director of Campus Planning.
The Advisory Board will consist of six at-large members and representatives from at least the following organizations:
Residential Student Association; and
Graduate Student Government.

DINING ADVISORY BOARD
The Dining Advisory has the power and responsibility to:
Serve as the official liaison between the Northeastern University student body and Northeastern University Dining Services, such as the Director of Campus Dining.
The Advisory Board will consist of six at-large members and representatives from at least the following organizations:
Slow Food NU;
Residential Student Association; and
Graduate Student Government.

ARTICLE V: DIVERSITY, EQUITY, AND INCLUSION
DIVISION RESPONSIBILITY
The Division of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion serves to address student concerns in the areas of diversity, equity, and inclusion, as well as to provide resources and opportunities for Northeastern students and student organizations in order to represent the diversity, equity, and inclusion interests of the Northeastern community.
The Division is to be an active advocate for marginalized communities at Northeastern University and shall serve to promote and improve social equity by collaborating with university staff, students, clubs, and departments.
The Division will advise the Executive Board and all Committees and Boards to:
Ensure the principles of diversity and inclusion are upheld in Association projects and legislation;
Provide all members of the Association with educational materials and training related to diversity and inclusion;
Hold a minimum of one diversity and inclusion workshop and/or collaborate with a student/outside organization to hold a workshop for all members of the Student Senate;
Collaborate with the Vice President of External Affairs, Director of Communications, and other relevant officers to increase communication and engagement with marginalized communities that are underrepresented in the Association; and
Work with other members of the Association Leadership to incorporate the principles of diversity and inclusion in all Association events.
The Division will be overseen by the Vice President of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion.

DIVERSITY, EQUITY, AND INCLUSION COMMITTEE
The Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion Committee will be chaired by the Director of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion
The Committee has the power and responsibility to:
Advance Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion initiatives by addressing student concerns, collaborating with organizations and cultural centers on accessibility and representation through outreach and forums, elevating issues to the administration, and organizing diversity, equity, and inclusion events and awareness campaigns.

INTERNAL ADVANCEMENT BOARD
The Internal Advancement Board will be chaired by the Vice President of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion.
The Board has the power and responsibility to:
Develop, implement, and uphold equitable internal association policies and practices including navigating confidential internal concerns that fall outside the scope of Title IX.
Strengthen equitable recruitment and leadership pipelines by developing a strategic plan with liaisons from each division, ensuring fair application review, and partnering with the Director of Events for targeted outreach;
Assess and enhance Association culture, retention, and inclusion by conducting annual demographic and experience surveys, developing action plans, and implementing retention strategies; and
Develop and implement standardized diversity, equity, and inclusion-focused Association-wide training and onboarding programs while maintaining a library of diversity, equity, and inclusion resources for Association Committees.
The Board should consist, at maximum, of eight members.

ARTICLE VI: EXTERNAL AFFAIRS
DIVISION RESPONSIBILITIES
The Division of External Affairs serves to enhance the visibility, influence, and outreach of the Association both within the Northeastern community and beyond. This Division manages the communication of Association projects, initiatives, and events to the student body, media outlets, alumni, and intercollegiate partners.
The Division will be overseen by the Vice President of External Affairs.

COMMUNICATIONS AND EVENTS COMMITTEE
The Communications and Events Committee will be co-chaired by the Director of Communications and the Director of Events.
The Committee has the power and responsibility to:
Create and curate content for the Association’s social media platforms to promote events, initiatives, and announcements;
Document Association activities and membership through photography & videography for external, internal, and archival purposes;
Maintain an Association brand guide and distribute branded materials to enhance the Association’s visibility and strengthen its identity across campus; and
Organize and execute school spirit, civic engagement, internal Association, recruitment, and other events.

SPECIALIZED LEADERSHIP POSITIONS
The Press Manager has the power and responsibility to:
Serve as a liaison between the Association and external media outlets;
Develop and distribute the Association’s Monthly Newsletter to the undergraduate student body to keep the student body informed about the Association’s work, upcoming events, and key accomplishments; and
Collaborate with the Vice President of External Affairs to draft press releases, coordinate media interviews and op-eds, and manage the Association’s public statements to ensure consistent, strategic messaging.
The Alumni Manager has the power and responsibility to:
Foster meaningful connections between current Association members and alumni;
Organize events such as networking receptions, speaker series, and mentorship programs, to strengthen alumni involvement; and
Cultivate relationships with alumni for potential sponsorship opportunities, career development initiatives, and support for Association programs.
The Boston Intercollegiate Government Representatives (2) have the power and responsibility to:
Serve as liaisons to the Boston Intercollegiate Government, a coalition of student governments from universities across the Boston area;
Be voting members of the Boston Intercollegiate Government General Assembly;
Communicate updates from the Boston Intercollegiate Government General Assembly meetings to the Vice President of External Affairs and advocate for Northeastern’s interests within the Boston Intercollegiate Government; and
Gather insights, best practices, and innovative ideas from peer institutions to enhance the Association’s projects and initiatives.

ARTICLE VII: STUDENT INVOLVEMENT
DIVISION RESPONSIBILITIES
The Division of Student Involvement serves to address Association needs in all matters, policies, and objectives pertaining to student organizations. Student Involvement will also advise the Center for Student Involvement on all matters pertaining to student organizations.
The Division will be overseen by the Vice President of Student Involvement.
The Division of Student Involvement will compile a yearly report summarizing initiatives, projects, and relevant information for the student body to be aware of. This report will include a summary of the decisions each Board in the Division made in the last year. This report must be submitted to the Director of Communications and distributed to student organization leaders.
The Director of the Center for Student Involvement or their designees may serve as an ex-officio non-voting member on all Boards within the Division.

STUDENT ORGANIZATION OPERATIONS COMMITTEE
The Student Organization Operations Committee will be chaired by the Director of Student Organization Operations.
The Committee has the power and responsibility to:
Encourage communication and collaboration between student organizations both within Northeastern Boston and the greater Northeastern global network;
Collaborate with various university administrators and departments related to resources available to student organizations; and
Undertake projects related to recommending changes or improvements to resources and processes available to student organizations.

STUDENT ORGANIZATION APPROVALS BOARD
The Student Organization Approvals Board will be chaired by the Director of Student Organization Approvals.
The Board has the power and responsibility to:
Oversee the new organization recognition and approval process; 
Guide and support new student organizations through the preliminary and tentative recognition processes;
Educate new student organization leaders on the policies and best practices of student organizations;
Review new student organization’s suitability to become a fully recognized student organization;
Hear presentations from tentative organizations and adjudicate a decision on their recognition status; and
Understand and enact the policies of the Student Organization Approvals and Recognition Manual.
The Board should consist, at maximum, of twelve members.

STUDENT ORGANIZATION COMPLIANCE BOARD
The Student Organization Compliance Board will be chaired by the Director of Student Organization Compliance.
The Board has the power and responsibility to:
Serve as an arbiter in matters in which a student feels that a student organization or its Executive Board is not following its governing documents;
Conduct investigations into student organizations suspected to be in non-compliance with their governing documents or Association policies, in collaboration with the Student Organization Finance Board in matters pertaining to the Student Activity Fee;
Request information, interviews, document, or other relevant materials pertaining to investigations;
Impose sanctions for failure to comply with requests;
Administer sanctions to student organizations it deems non-compliant with their governing documents, or Association policies including, but not limited to:
Written warnings;
Revocation of specific privileges of being fully recognized;
Revocation or modification of organization recognition status;
Refer student organizations to the Student Organization Finance Board; and
Refer relevant parties to the University’s Office of Student Conduct and Conflict Resolution (OSCCR).
Educate organizations, their officers, and their members on relevant university and Association policies as a remediation method;
Refer student organizations, their officers, or their members to university administration for further investigation in situations where non-compliance with university policies is suspected;
Foster a culture of student organizations understanding and complying with Association policies;
Oversee all investigations into student organization non-compliance of Association policy; and
Receive, triage, and address all feedback and complaint submission pertaining to student organizations in collaboration with university administration.
The Board should consist, at maximum, of six members.

STUDENT ORGANIZATION FINANCE BOARD
The Student Organization Finance Board will be chaired by the Director of Student Organization Finance.
The Board has the power and responsibility to:
Allocate the Student Activity Fee to Association-recognized student organizations;
Allocate the Student Activity Fee to campus offices for critical campus-wide programming for undergraduate students;
Collect and review spending reports, according to deadlines established in the Student Activity Fee Manual;
Conduct investigations into student organizations suspected to be in non-compliance with financial policy in collaboration with the Student Organization Compliance Board;
Serve as the liaison between students and the Student Activities Business Office; and
Understand and enact the policies of the Student Activity Fee Manual.
The Board should consist, at maximum, of twelve members.

STUDENT ORGANIZATION GOVERNANCE BOARD
The Student Organization Governance Board will be chaired by the Director of Student Organization Governance.
The Board has the power and responsibility to:
Educate organizations, their executive board, and their members on relevant university and Association policies;
Review and update Association policies related to student organizations to promote fairness, clarity, and inclusivity through Association legislation;
Oversee the student organization constitution revision and approval process; 
Review and approve changes in student organization constitutions; and
Understand and enact the policies of the Student Organization Policy Manual.
The Board should consist, at maximum, of six members.

ARTICLE X: STANDING BODIES

Chair meetings;
Set the date, time, place and agenda for each meeting;
Maintain meeting minutes;
Maintain the roster;
Manage attendance records;
Coordinate all administrative duties of the Standing Body;
Maintain communication with the Vice President of the relevant division; and

All members of a Standing Body will have the power and responsibility to:

ARTICLE XI: BOARDS
RESPONSIBILITIES
Boards serve as the official decision-making bodies for related topics.
Boards will meet weekly as often as possible throughout the semester.

MEMBERSHIP
The Board’s membership will be comprised of the following:
A defined amount of voting members; and
The Chair of the Board as a non-voting member.

VOTING
Quorum will be considered half of the voting members of the Board plus one.
In the case of a tie, the Chair will cast the deciding vote.
When an inherent conflict exists, a member must abstain from voting.

ARTICLE XII: ADVISORY BOARDS
RESPONSIBILITIES
Advisory Boards serve as the official representative of student interests as they relate to their mission and act as a liaison between the Northeastern student body and relevant Northeastern administration.
Advisory Boards will meet at least once per month with their administrative contacts.
Meeting agendas will be set collaboratively.
The Advisory Board will maintain the confidentiality of administrative initiatives.
Meetings will be closed but guests may be invited at the discretion of the Chair and administrative contacts.
Advisory Boards will assist in ongoing student outreach initiatives, including, but not limited to: tabling, surveys, and forums.
Advisory Boards will prepare viewpoints and positions to discuss with the relevant administration in accordance with their responsibility as a representative body.
Advisory Boards will meet independently and regularly to discuss ongoing student concerns and priorities.

MEMBERSHIP
The Advisory Board’s membership will be comprised of the following: 
A defined amount of, at-large, voting members, one of which will act as the Chair; and
Up to five voting representatives from selected Northeastern constituencies, as selected by their constituencies’ leadership.
Each representative will serve a term from July 1 through June 30.
The Advisory Board can vote to add additional non-voting representatives up to a maximum of five.
Any person may be invited by the Chair to attend regular Advisory Board meetings as a non-voting member.
The Chair of an Advisory Board will maintain the rights and privileges of a member of the Advisory Board.

APPLICATIONS & ELECTIONS
Membership for at-large seats on the Advisory Board will occur in two cycles per year.
Half of the at-large members (rounded down) will be elected for a term of January 1 through December 31.
Half of the at-large members (rounded up) will be elected for a term of July 1 through June 30.
Applications will open approximately a month prior to the end of each semester.
Applications will be vetted by the Chair before review and selection by the Advisory Board.
If the Chair is applying for renewed membership, a separate voting member of the Advisory Board, who is not applying for renewed membership, will be selected to oversee the application process.
Following the election of at-large, the Advisory Board will select a Chair.
The Chair will be one of the sitting at-large members.
Any member may nominate any eligible candidate, including themselves.
Each Chair will serve a six-month term.
A member may only serve as Chair for a maximum of two terms.
This requirement is waived if there is no qualifying member of the Advisory Board willing to be nominated.
If the Chair resigns or is unable to continue their term, an emergency election will be held.
Members who miss three consecutive meetings or five meetings within an eight-week period are subject to dismissal at the discretion of the Advisory Board.
The Chair may propose candidates to fill an open seat on the Advisory Board outside of the application cycle. This candidate will be confirmed by the Advisory Board prior to membership.

VOTING
Quorum will be considered half of the voting members of the Advisory Board plus one.
When an inherent conflict exists, a member must abstain from voting.

Working Groups serve as the official representative of student interests as they relate to their mission and act as a bridge between key student constituencies and relevant Northeastern administration.

Ensure representatives’ input on current topics from the perspective of their constituency.

ARTICLE XIV: SENATE
SENATOR CONSTITUENCIES
Senators must be enrolled as undergraduate students of Northeastern University, in good academic and judicial standing, and be responsible to their constituencies. Senators must hold two constituencies: an academic constituency and a Community Constituency.
Senators must have been elected by their Academic Constituency. 
Senators are responsible for maintaining an effective two-way communication system with and consistently returning to and representing their constituents.
An academic constituency is the academic college or program that the Senator is representing.
A Community Constituency is an organization that a Senator actively participates in and advocates for in the Senate. A Community Constituency is intended to show a candidate’s involvement on campus and lived experience. All Community Constituency organizations must be approved or pre-approved by the Association.
All Association recognized student organizations are pre-approved to be community constituencies.
All Association recognized student organizations may only have one Candidate for Senatorship per Academic Constituency.
All of the Northeastern Cultural and Spiritual Life Centers are pre-approved to be community constituencies. All student organizations housed within the Cultural and Spiritual Life Centers are pre-approved to be Community Constituencies.
All Center for Student Involvement recognized Fraternities and Sororities are pre-approved to be Community Constituencies.
All Center for Student Involvement recognized Fraternities and Sororities may only have one Candidate for Senatorship per Academic Constituency.
If a potential Senator wishes to represent a different organization, they may petition the Speaker of the Senate to approve it. The Speaker of the Senate may approve it under the following conditions:
The organization is affiliated with Northeastern or comprised of over 50% Northeastern students; and
The organization does not promote values or take part in actions that go against the Association’s values.
The Association can not be a Community Constituency.

SENATOR REQUIREMENTS
Senators are required to attend weekly Senate meetings, attend all Full Body Senate meetings, act as liaisons between the Association and their respective constituencies, and, if decided by the Speaker of the Senate, complete engagement activities with their constituencies.
Senators must attend at least one form of the Senator Training and Education Program, conducted by the Speaker of the Senate. The training must include instruction on, but is not limited to:
Parliamentary Procedure and Robert’s Rules of Order in coordination with the Parliamentarian;
Their responsibilities to their constituents and to the Association;
The leadership structure of the Association and its Committee and Boards;
Media training on how best to interact with press in coordination with the Vice President of External Affairs;
Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion training in coordination with the Vice President of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion; and
A basic training on how to create and present legislation.
Any Senator who fails to complete the Senator Training and Education Program, after being offered a reasonable opportunity to do so, will be removed from the roster at the discretion of the Speaker of the Senate.

SENATOR ATTENDANCE
A Senator may be excused from a Senate or Full Body Senate meeting for one of the following reasons, provided a 24-hour notice is given to the Speaker of the Senate or their designee(s):
An academic commitment;
Personal illness or injury;
Illness or death in the family;
Unavoidable occupational commitment;
Religious or cultural commitment;
Jury duty;
Military service; or
A reason approved by the Speaker of the Senate.
In extenuating circumstances, the Speaker of the Senate has the option to waive the 24-hour requirement.
Any Senator attending less than one half of a Senate or Full Body Senate meeting will be assessed one half of an absence.
Any Senator accumulating three or more unexcused absences from weekly Senate meetings or one unexcused absence from a Full Body Senate meeting in one semester will be deemed by the Speaker of the Senate to have abandoned the seat and will be removed from the roster.
Any Senator may request a leave of absence from the Speaker of the Senate if they will not be present in Boston, be unable to attend remotely, and be unable to attend multiple consecutive meetings. If it is granted, the Senator will not receive any unexcused absences during the period of leave. A leave of absence should be equal to or less than 8 weeks. If a Senator will not be able to attend for 9 or more weeks, the Speaker of the Senate may declare the seat vacant.
Any Senator may request virtual meeting attendance from the Speaker of the Senate with 24-hour notice. To be able to attend a weekly Senate or Full Body Senate meeting virtually said Senator must be able to hear and speak in all discussions and view all pertinent documents.

SENATE MEETINGS
Senate meetings are open to the public except for executive sessions.
The Executive Board shall be responsible for providing pertinent documents, to be reviewed and considered by the Senate, to the entire Full Body Senate roster, no later than 48 hours prior to the Senate’s call to order for all legislation, and 24 hours for all other documents.
All members of the Executive Board must attend all Full Body Senate meetings.
If they are unable to attend, they must notify the Speaker of the Senate one week in advance. Exceptions can be made at the discretion of the Speaker of the Senate. If they are not able to attend, they must send a designee in their place.
In circumstances where the Senate is not meeting regularly for an extended period of time due to an academic recess, such as during winter or summer break, the Executive Board may call an Emergency Recess meeting of the Senate to pass legislation on behalf of the student body.
A simple majority vote of the Executive Board will be required to call an Emergency Recess meeting based on the following criteria:
A lack of immediate action by the Senate would negatively impact the student body to a severe and significant degree.
Such actions could not reasonably be delayed until the Senate resumes regular weekly meeting times.
Quorum for an Emergency Recess meeting will be defined as one-third of the voting roster, plus one. Only members with voting rights will count toward quorum at any time.
All legislation for an Emergency Recess meeting will be introduced under New Business, and will be voted on during the same meeting.
The Executive Board will give no fewer than one week of notice to the Senate roster as to the time and place of the meeting. An Emergency Recess meeting can be called only if there is adequate time to notify the Senate roster.
The Executive Board shall provide all pertinent documents to be reviewed and considered by the Senate no later than one week prior to the Senate’s call to order of the Emergency Recess meeting.

VOTING PROCEDURES 
All votes concerning legislation and substantive amendments will be taken by roll-call votes unless a motion is made from the floor for adoption by unanimous consent. If no objection is raised to the motion, the legislation or amendment is considered ratified. If an objection is raised, a roll-call vote must be taken.
Roll-call votes are not mandatory for approval of minutes, or for any other motions and non-substantive amendments to legislation.
Senators maintain the right to call for a roll call vote at times that they deem appropriate and that are in accordance with Robert’s Rules of Order, Newly Revised.

ARTICLE XV: NOMINATIONS AND ELECTIONS 
ELECTION REQUIREMENTS
A candidate for President must be a matriculated undergraduate student in good academic standing.
A candidate for Executive Vice President must have been an active member of the Association for twenty-five weeks prior to their nomination meeting.
A candidate for Speaker of the Senate must have been a Senator for twenty-five weeks prior to their nomination.
A candidate for a Vice President position must have been an active member within the relevant division for twenty weeks prior to their nomination.
A candidate for Director of Elections must have been an Elections Board Member for fifteen weeks prior to their nomination.
A candidate for Director of Student Organization Finance must have been a Finance Board Member for fifteen weeks prior to their nomination.
If a candidate is nominated for a position which they are not eligible to hold because of a subsection within this section, at the close of nominations, any Senator may make a motion to suspend the aforementioned section. The motion requires a simple majority vote in the Senate to pass.

PRESIDENTIAL ELECTION
Annual elections for the President and Executive Vice President of the following academic year will be held in the spring semester according to procedures in the Presidential Elections Manual.
Any member of the Senate may move to block any nomination for a directly elected position during any Full Body Senate at which nominations are made.
The motion to block will be carried by a two-thirds vote, conducted by secret ballot.
The Senate may enter into an executive session to debate on the merits of blocking a candidate with a simple majority vote following a motion made by a Senator.
The Senate may invite one or more candidates to attend part or all of the executive session through a motion made by a Senator and carried by a simple majority vote, otherwise no candidates for directly elected positions may attend the executive session or have voting rights on the secret ballot.

INTERNAL ELECTIONS
Any candidate for an internally-elected position must have a nomination from a Senator or member of the Executive Board and have that nomination seconded by an additional Senator.
In order to nominate a candidate, the Senator must inform the Director of Elections and Speaker of the Senate of:
The full name of the candidate; and
The position the candidate is being nominated for.
In order to second a candidate, the additional Senator must approve the previous Senator’s nomination of a candidate.
The candidate must accept the nomination prior to their election.
In an Internal Election, a Senator may: 
Vote for any candidate qualified and nominated as prescribed by the Constitution and Bylaws;
Submit a statement of no confidence in all candidates nominated for that position; or 
Abstain from voting. 
The winner of an election will be determined by plurality vote.
If this vote results in a vote of “no confidence”, the position will be declared vacant and nominations for the position will reopen. A special election will be duly convened. If no qualified members have accepted nominations by the close of nominations, or if the special election otherwise fails to produce an officer, the Executive Board will appoint a Senator or Delegate to serve as the acting officer until a special election can convene to fill the position. 
Students are ineligible for office if they are on track to graduate at the end of the fall semester.
The internal election process will follow a procedure recommended by the Elections Board and approved by the Executive Board.

SENATE ELECTIONS
Semesterly elections for the available Senate seats will be held at the beginning of each semester according to the procedures in the Senate Elections Manual. 
Seats will be up for election, in general, on a cycling basis. Approximately half of available seats for each academic constituency will be available each semester with the other half being available the following semester.
An Applicant for Senatorship must complete the application that is outlined in the Senate Elections Manual and provided by the Association.
Senator terms will be one calendar year. Senators' terms will begin with the announcement of their election results and their term will end with the announcement of next calendar year’s election results announcement. Elections will occur in both Spring and Fall semesters. 
If a Senator vacates their office prior to the end of their term and is replaced, their replacement may serve for shorter than one calendar year.

ARTICLE XVI: SENATE LEGISLATION 
The following forms of legislation will be used by the Senate. 
A Senate Resolution will be used to call for action from the University on any matter that affects the student body directly or indirectly. These resolutions are forwarded to the President of the University for consideration.
A Sense of the Senate will be used to provide student opinion on any matter of concern to students.
An Internal Resolution will be used to temporarily amend the Constitution or Bylaws. An Internal Resolution will expire at the close of the Summer I semester following its adoption, unless the resolution specifies an expiration date that is before the close of the Summer I semester following its adoption.
A Resolution of Governance will be used to permanently amend one or more governing documents.

The Speaker of the Senate shall have the power to assign passed legislation to any division within the Association to ensure its execution.

Any Internal Resolution or Resolution of Governance that strictly addresses amendments to Internal Manuals or the Bylaws will be presented to and voted upon by the Executive Board.
The decision of the Executive Board must be an agenda item at the next Senate meeting.
The Senate may choose to override the decision of the Executive Board with a simple majority.
Should the Senate not override the decision, the Executive Board’s decision is applied to the Resolution with the authority of the Senate.

If legislation ratified in the Senate is not approved by the signature of the Association President within one week, the legislation is enacted. If vetoed, the reasons for the veto must be presented by the President at the next Senate meeting.

All legislation will be introduced to the Senate under New Business. This legislation can only be voted upon during the next meeting or any subsequent meeting, in which the resolution is introduced under Old Business. The Bylaws can be suspended in accordance with the rules for the suspension of ordinary standing rules in Robert’s Rules of Order, Newly Revised. In order for this rule to be suspended, a two-thirds majority will be required for the suspension of the rule and the passage of the legislation.

Legislation pending on the floor of the Senate at the end of any semester must be reintroduced in the following semester. Any amendments or changes in language or structure approved during the previous semester are retained, even if the amended document was not voted on in the previous semester. Standing Senate rules that apply to new legislation will also apply to reintroduced legislation. If the chief sponsor of the legislation to be reintroduced will not be present, the sponsor will be responsible for officially appointing a new chief sponsor for the legislation.

ARTICLE XVII: MAINTENANCE OF PUBLIC RECORDS 
Student Government Association Senate minutes must be posted to an easily accessible public forum no more than five business days after the approval of the minutes.


Student Government Association legislation must be posted to an easily accessible public forum no more than five business days after the legislation is signed by the President, vetoed, or passed by the overturning of a Presidential veto.


Non-confidential Standing Body reports or major proceedings must be posted to an easily accessible public forum after their approval or presentation to the Association Senate.


Meeting minutes, legislation, reports, and other documents substantively documenting the work of the Association may not be destroyed. Minor documents will be defined as documents not meeting the above criteria and may be discarded at any time.


All non-confidential records of the Student Government Association will be made available to members of the University community upon request within seven days after the request is made. Should a request be made during a recess or intersession, the request will be fulfilled seven days after classes resume following the recess or intersession during which the request is made.


Any non-confidential document may be archived with the University library consistent with their practices and policies. Any request for documents that have been archived at the library will result in the requester being notified that the records are in the library within seven days of the request, and the remainder of the request will not be covered by the Association’s procedures. Records may also be archived internally.


If a records request cannot be met within seven days due to the size or availability of records, the requester shall be notified of the time delay, and a new timeline for release shall be agreed upon. If the document is permanently unavailable, the requester shall be notified within seven days.


If a records request is made for a confidential document, the requester shall be notified within seven days.


Public records requests will be the responsibility of the Vice President of Operational Affairs or designee.

ARTICLE XVIII: STANDING PROCEDURES 
The standing procedures of the Association will be a document that may contain parliamentary procedures not specified in the Constitution or Bylaws. The standing procedures will be subordinate to the Constitution and Bylaws, amenable by a simple majority of a Full Body Senate. 

ARTICLE XIX: ASSOCIATION APPROVAL 
This amended document has been approved by the required two-thirds majority vote of the Full Body Senate of the Association.
