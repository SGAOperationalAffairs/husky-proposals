CONSTITUTION  

ARTICLE I: NAME

The name of this organization will be the Student Government Association of Northeastern University. For the purposes of this document, the organization will be referred to as the Association.  

ARTICLE II: MISSION

MISSION STATEMENT  
The Student Government Association serves as the voice of the undergraduate student body.  
It strives to promote student interests within the University and its surrounding communities in order to enrich education, student life, and the overall Northeastern experience. 

OBJECT
In support of this mission, the object of the Association is to be the official liaison between the students and the  University Faculty and Administration; to act in an advisory capacity to the Faculty and Administration; to strive to make the University a continually more serviceable institution; to serve students as a resource in resolving concerns; to be the means by which the student body of the University communicates with the faculty, administration, and Trustees of the University; to be the approval authority for all petitions or referenda that students or student organizations wish to circulate among the student body; to be the means by which students influence the educational programs, processes, and the administrative management of the University;  and to distribute funds from the Student Activity Fee to eligible student organizations. The Association is the means by which students are represented in the governance of the University. The Association is therefore the general interest group on campus advocating in the interest of all undergraduate students. At its electoral base are full-time undergraduate students currently enrolled in the University. The Association’s operational activities are carried out by the Senate, Executive Board, and Operation Appeals Board, which are open to all qualified students in the manner prescribed by the Constitution.
The Association was established on the basis that it would be the representative group of the entire full time undergraduate University community including all of its diverse views and peoples. The Association will not discriminate on the basis of race, ethnicity, national origin, color, gender, gender identity, religion, religious creed, genetics, age, sex (including pregnancy or pregnancy-related condition), ancestry, sexual orientation, disability, or veteran status.
It is the hope and duty of the Association that it respects, understands, and accepts the differences and similarities that exist within and outside the University community.

ARTICLE III: SENATE

SENATE  
The Senate is the deliberative body from which all authority vested in the Association originates. The Senate quorum shall be one-half of the voting roster, plus one. Only members with voting rights will count toward quorum at any time. The Senate has the powers and responsibilities to:
Represent the voice of the student body;
Pass legislation to recommend actions to the Office of Student Affairs regarding University life,  policies, and concerns of the student body;
Override an executive veto or decision by a two-thirds majority;
Approve the Executive Board’s budget proposal and recommend it for submission to the Finance Board for final approval;
Approve or disapprove requests by political candidates to obtain access to the University in the absence of other recognition from sponsoring organizations or units within the University (this decision is subject to University policies and procedures governing access to the campus of outside individuals or  organizations);
Remove any officer from the Association, following the impeachment process set forth in the Bylaws;
Retain all powers vested in the Association that are not specifically delegated in the Constitution and Bylaws;
Approve any allocation from the cash account regarding Association stipends, at the recommendation of the Student Body President;
Act as the approval body of outside individuals or organizations to circulate petitions or referenda among the students in accordance with the procedures set forth in the Bylaws.

FULL BODY SENATE
Full Body meetings of the Senate represent the full membership of the Senate.  
Full Body Senate meetings, in addition to possessing all powers of the Senate, are held to:
Open nominations for and elect Association officers designated for Senate election;
Confirm or reject any officers appointed by the Student Body President, the Executive Vice President, a Vice President, the Student Body President-Elect, the Executive Vice President-Elect, or a Vice President-Elect
Make changes to the Association Constitution and Bylaws;  
Hear and decide charges of impeachment.  
Full Body Senate meetings can be called by the Executive Board or by a simple majority of the Senate, initiated by a motion from a Senator. A Full Body meeting can be called only if there is adequate time to notify the Senate roster.  
The Executive Board will give no fewer than three weeks of notice to the Full Body  Senate roster as to the time and place of the meeting
Only one week of notice is required for a meeting to hear impeachment charges or hold a special election
A two-thirds majority of the total voting membership of Senate can call a Full Body meeting with only one week of notice for any purpose.  
Quorum for the Full Body Senate meeting will be one-half of the full voting roster plus one.

SENATORS  
Members of the Senate must be enrolled as full-time, undergraduate students of Northeastern University, in good academic and judicial standing, and be responsible to an identifiable constituency. Senators are responsible for maintaining an effective system of two-way communication and consistently returning to and representing their respective constituents. Senators will serve terms that each span one calendar year beginning on September 30th for senators beginning their terms in a Fall Semester or January 31st for senators beginning their terms in a Spring Semester.

VACANCIES
All vacancies when an elected or appointed officer resigns, is removed from office, or is declared by a vote of the Executive Board to have abandoned their office must be made public on the SGA website within 24 hours and the entire Senate roster must be notified as well.  
Emergency Elections
During the fall or summer semesters, if an elected officer resigns, is removed from, or abandons office, the Executive Board must call a Full Body Senate meeting as soon as possible to carry out an emergency election.
All elected positions, even those normally directly elected by the student body will be elected by a Full Body Senate in an emergency election.
Nominations by mail for the vacant position will open automatically as soon as the vacancy is publicly announced. This must be indicated when the Senate roster is notified of a vacancy.
If an elected officer resigns, is removed from, or abandons office during the spring semester, there will be no emergency election unless there is not a successor designated to assume the position.
If an appointed officer resigns, is removed from, or abandons office, the appointing officer must appoint a replacement within two weeks and then the Executive Board must call a Full Body Senate meeting as soon as possible to carry out the confirmation which will be considered an emergency election.
Succession
If the President resigns, is removed from, or abandons office, the Executive Vice President will permanently assume the presidency and temporarily retain the position of Executive Vice  President until a new Executive Vice President is elected in an emergency election.
If the Executive Vice President resigns, is removed from, or abandons office, the Executive Board will appoint a Vice President to serve as the presiding officer of Senate until a new Executive Vice President is elected in an emergency election.
If both the President and Executive Vice President resign, are removed from, or abandon their offices, officers in the following order, unless vacant, will temporarily assume the presidency and serve as the presiding officer of Senate until a new President and Executive Vice President are elected in an emergency election: Vice President for Operational Affairs, Vice President for Academic Affairs, Vice President for Campus Affair, Vice President for External Affairs, Vice President for Student Involvement, and Vice President for Student Success. Only duly elected Vice Presidents will be eligible to assume the position of President.
If any elected officer other than the President or Executive Vice President resigns, is removed  from, or abandons office:
In the fall or summer semesters, their designated successor will temporarily assume the elected office until an emergency election can be held to replace the officer.
In the spring semester, their appropriate successor will assume the elected office for the remainder of the elected officer’s term.
All Vice Presidents will designate a successor and inform the Executive Vice President of their choice. The successor must be a student who is involved with the Association, eligible to hold the position, and willing to temporarily assume the Vice Presidency. Vice Presidents are responsible for having a valid successor whenever possible.
In any case where there is no person designated to temporarily serve in a vacant elected position, the Executive Board will appoint a temporary officer from the Senate Roster to serve until a new officer can be elected in an emergency election.

ARTICLE IV: EXECUTIVE BOARD

EXECUTIVE BOARD
The Executive Board is the executive authority of the Association. The Student Body President will chair the  Executive Board and votes only in the case of a tie.
The Executive Board is composed of the
Student Body President;
Executive Vice President;
Speaker of the Senate;
Vice President for Academic Affairs;
Vice President for Campus Affairs;
Vice President for Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion;
Vice President for External Affairs;
Vice President for Operational Affairs;
Vice President for Student Involvement; and
Vice President for Student Success.
The Executive Board collectively serves the student body and has the powers and responsibilities to: 
Set the date, time, place, and agenda of Senate meetings;
Provide the agenda of the Executive Board on the Association’s website, within 24 hours of  the  Executive Board meeting;
Confirm students to the Finance Board, the Green Initiatives Finance Board, the Student  Organization Approvals Board, Student Organization Governance Board, Student Organization Complicate Board, and the Elections Board;
Chairs of their respective boards must be present for and have a vote in confirmations to their board
Confirm students for Senate consideration of appointment to the Operational Appeals  Board;
Vote on questions of whether Association officers have abandoned duties, evaluate the  performance of Association officers, and make recommendations to the Senate concerning  their individual continuations in office;
Act for the Senate when it is not possible to wait for the next Senate meeting for a  decision on an issue. The Executive Board shall not have the authority when the issue involves major policy changes or adaptations that will significantly affect the student body. When the Executive Board acts on behalf of the Senate, the Senate shall be  informed of such actions and the reasons for them immediately;
Act for a committee or board when a committee or board may not meet. Acting in this capacity, the Executive Board will make every effort to contact the members of the appropriate committee or board to determine the status of the issue when the committee or board last met;
Represent the Association to the Board of Trustees, University President, and other senior  University officials concerning matters related to the Undergraduate Student Body;
Create or eliminate positions within the Association that are not enumerated in the  Constitution and Bylaws and do not alter the voting membership of the Senate;
Approve all purchases that exceed one hundred dollars out of either the Association’s budget or cash accounts accounts;
Inform the student body through reports by each member of the board submitted at the end of each month of the semester on initiatives, projects, and relevant information for the student body to be aware of. They must submit these monthly reports to the Director of Communications;
Members of the Executive Board may be privy to the confidential information generated by the association, but are required to hold any information received in strict confidence.

EXECUTIVE POSITIONS
STUDENT BODY PRESIDENT
The Student Body President (also “President”) is the Chief Executive Officer of the Association and is responsible for executing all its policies and objectives. The President oversees external Association communications, interactions with community bodies and Boston-area student governance bodies. Over the course of Academic semesters, the President is expected to work approximately 40 hours per week in carrying out their duties. The President has the powers and  responsibilities to:
Be the spokesperson for the Association, and the student body as a whole;
Chair the Executive Board;
Report to the Senate on Executive Board actions and activities;
Serve as a non-voting member of the Senate;
Approve or veto legislation with a signature within one week of Senate approval;
Appoint student members to represent the Association to University committees with a  simple majority approval of the Executive Board;
Receive reports from the student members of University committees and, if necessary, report  to the Senate of these meetings;
Facilitate the Association’s involvement with University-wide financial planning and  priorities;
Receive reports on and make recommendations for the allocation of all student controlled  fees, where a board has not been established to oversee such allocations;
Nominate Justices for the Operational Appeals Board, and release the names of the nominated candidates prior to confirmation by the Senate and the Executive Board;
Evaluate the performance of the Association officers, and make recommendations through the  Executive Board to the Senate concerning their individual continuations in office;
Work with the Vice President for Operational Affairs to develop the Association’s budget proposal for the next fiscal  year, subject to Executive Board approval, and submit it to the Senate;
Act as a liaison notwithstanding any liaison authority possessed by other Association officers;
Oversee Association procedures related to Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion in conjunction with the Vice President for Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion; and
Oversee the work of all Executive Directors and the Parliamentarian.

EXECUTIVE VICE PRESIDENT
The Executive Vice President serves to assist the President in long-term planning, special projects and initiatives. In addition, the Executive Vice President oversees the Association’s internal communications, fundraising, and alumni connections.  Over the course of Academic semesters the Executive Vice President is expected to work approximately 30 hours per week in carrying out their duties. The Executive Vice President has the  powers and responsibilities to:
Oversee the work of all Vice Presidents and the Speaker of the Senate;
Serve as a non-voting member of the Senate;  
Assist the Student Body President in fulfilling their duties and completing special projects intended to support the interests of the student body at large;  
Oversee internal operations of the organization alongside the Vice President for Operational Affairs and assist in the creation of the Association’s Budget proposal for the next fiscal year;  
Inform the members of the Executive Board of any pending expenditures over one hundred dollars;  
Assist in liaising between Association officers and advisors of the Association and/or staff of Student life;  
Chair Senate meetings in the case that the Speaker of the Senate is unable to fulfill their duties due to external commitments or a conflict of interest with an item of the Senate agenda;  
Assist the Student Body President in the evaluation of Association officers and hold check-in meetings with officers as needed;
Chair Executive Board meetings as delegated by the Student Body President, in which the Executive Vice President shall forfeit  their voting rights except in the event of a tie;  
Appoint students-at-large to ad-hoc positions to support the roles and responsibilities of the Executive Vice President.

SPEAKER OF THE SENATE
The Speaker of the Senate serves as the presiding officer of the Senate and votes only in the case of a tie. Additionally, the Speaker of the Senate will be responsible for chairing the Senate, overseeing all Senate communications, Senate events, Senator recruitment, and Senator education. The Speaker of the Senate is expected to work 25 hours a week in carrying out their duties, which include:
Be the presiding officer of the Senate, voting only in the case of a tie;
Oversee the work of Senators and continuously adapt Senate to fit the needs of the Senate Body and the Student Body;  
Adapt Senator training programs on a semesterly basis to ensure Senators are well educated on their responsibilities; 
Appoint students-at-large to ad-hoc positions to support the roles and responsibilities of the Speaker of the Senate.

For appeals of Finance Board decisions:
Quorum shall be no less than the full voting membership, and the Board will be composed of  the following individuals:
Executive Director of Club Finance
A Justice from the general membership of the Board;
The  University’s Senior Vice Chancellor for Student Affairs, or designee;
The Student Body President as a non-voting member; and
The Parliamentarian as a non-voting member;
If the Association is the appealing party, the University’s Senior Vice Chancellor for Student  Affairs, or designee, will make the final decision
For appeals of Student Involvement decisions:
An officer from the Division of Student Involvement relevant to the decision shall make available decision rationale to the Board at least 24 hours before the appeal.
Quorum shall be no less than the full voting membership and the Board will be composed of  the following individuals:
Vice President for Student Involvement;
A Justice from the general membership of the Board;
The Director of the Center for Student Involvement or designee;
Student Body President as a non-voting member;
Parliamentarian as a non-voting member;
For all other jurisdictions, the Board will be composed of six Justices with quorum being no less than four and the following, non-voting members:
The Parliamentarian;
A University administrator within the Division of Student Affairs to provide advice relevant  to a given case;
In appeals of board or committee decisions, the Student Body President.  

ARTICLE VI: PARLIAMENTARY AUTHORITY

The rules of order as established in Robert’s Rules of Order Newly Revised will govern the Association in all  cases in which they are applicable and not in conflict with any portion of this Constitution or any bylaws, rules, or procedures that the Association may adopt.  

ARTICLE VII: ADVISOR

The advisor to the Association will be a full-time faculty member of the University. A co-advisor shall be appointed by the Executive Board; the co-advisor shall be any full time staff member in the Division of Student  Affairs within the University. The advisors will have the responsibilities to:
Provide advice and guidance to the members of the Association on matters of importance to the  Association;
Serve as a conduit, when requested, between the Association and the Faculty and Administration of the  University;
Optionally oversee the counting of ballots at Association elections;
Serve as a repository of Association knowledge and provide this information when requested and as  necessary to the members of the Association; and
Serve as a non-voting member on all association boards and committees. Attendance at these meetings will not be considered mandatory unless agreed upon with the appropriate chairperson.
The advisor(s) will serve at the pleasure of the Executive Board.
 
ARTICLE VIII: AMENDMENTS

To amend this document, a Resolution of Governance, as defined by the Bylaws, must be ratified by a two-thirds vote of the Full Body Senate. Such Resolutions shall take effect once signed by the President of the University or their  designee.
